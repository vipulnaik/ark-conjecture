## 1. Lemma D2 falsified, then replaced by a domination theorem (was A18)

**What D2 used to say.** *Outside blocks are never fused, by any layer.* An orbit of F ≥ 2 fused outside blocks of prime size r was said to carry a same-position class of at most (F/2)·r pairs for even F and F·r for odd F, hence **m\* ≤ |O|/2 ≤ n/2**, hence such configurations could be excluded from the shape space outright. Its status line read "proved, and more strongly than first conjectured — the bound is m\* ≤ n/2 outright, so no threshold is needed".

**Why it was false.** The last step took the block-permuting group's minimum orbital on unordered pairs of blocks to be F/2 or F, which is the divisibility argument for a transitive group of **prime-power degree**. Under the corrected shape space the block count is F = F_mid·F_top and need not be a prime power, and the permuter need only be compatible with the chain — so it may be **2-homogeneous**, with pair-orbital C(F,2).

**The witness, verified.** The gap is realised by an actual Oliver group, not merely by an admissible permuter:

> **n = 85.** Five blocks of size 17; diagonal translation τ; diagonal twist of order 16 (r − 1 = 16 = 2⁴, so the whole multiplicative group is available inside a 2-group); **AGL(1,5) = C₅ ⋊ C₄** permuting the block indices, C₅ as a 5-cycle and C₄ as i ↦ 2i (mod 5).
>
> Machine-checked: |Γ| = 5440, Γ₁ = ⟨τ, c₅⟩ ≅ **C₈₅ cyclic and normal**, Γ/Γ₁ of order **64** (a 2-group), transitive on all 85 points. Orbitals computed exhaustively: **170 / 680 / 2720** (summing to C(85,2) = 3570), so **m\* = 170 = 2|O|** — four times the claimed bound of |O|/2 = 42.5. The binding class is exactly C(5,2)·17, so the corrected same-position bound is tight.

Vipul independently reviewed and verified the example and the theorem's proof.

**What replaced it.** The **fused-outside domination theorem**, now Lemma D2 in `enumeration-proof.md` Part D2: for r ≠ q, **m\* ≤ n·min(F, r)/2 ≤ n^{3/2}/2**, via a within-block class ≤ F·C(r,2) always and a same-position class ≤ C(F,2)·r when F < r. Two features of the proof are worth recording because nothing else in the framework had used them:

- **Sylow-r cyclicity.** With r ∉ {p, q} every r-element lands in the cyclic layer, so the Sylow r-subgroup is cyclic; with F < r this forces the r-elements to be exactly the pure translations and the translation group to be diagonal — a cleaner derivation of the diagonal step than the old "Γ₁/Γ₂ cannot contain C_r^F" argument, and one that survives an arbitrary permuter.
- **A coprime-cohomology normalisation.** Since r ∤ |Q| for Q = Γ|_O/T\*, H¹(Q, V) = 0 and complements are conjugate by a translation vector — i.e. by a change of per-block origins. This is what makes "same position" coordinate-independent; without it an element acting as (ax, ax + u) across two blocks merges the offset-zero class into offset u. The old proof never addressed this, and `mu_enumerate_v2.py`'s code comment had been assuming it silently.

**The exclusion is now a domination, with a range-scoped half.** A fused-outside configuration cannot be extremal wherever √n < δ(n)·(n − 1). With the ladder's δ ≥ 0.02516 on n ≤ 10⁶ that is a theorem for **n ≥ 1582**; below it, direct scoring of the bound against every tabulated value clears with worst ratio **0.8276 at n = 56** (F = 8, r = 7), no other value above 0.77. The two overlap, so the whole range to 10⁶ is covered; beyond it the exclusion needs only δ(n) ≫ n^{−1/2}, weaker than anything else the framework assumes.

**Consequences that landed in the documents.** No computed value of B(n) changes. S9 moved from "never exists (Lemma D2)" to "exists; dominated" — the first census row ever to make that move, and the reason the census's status column now distinguishes *excluded by theorem* from *occurs and loses*. Theorem 3.1's "foreign … and never fused" clause became "unfused, fusing being possible but dominated" in both DUP copies. Corollary D2′ was restated as what fusion of outside blocks costs rather than as an impossibility. `parts_for`'s comment in `mu_enumerate_v2.py` was corrected: it had justified the skip by a class of size ~F·c/2, which quotes a small permuter's pair-orbital and understates the class — the right figure is C(F,2)·c, and the skip is sound for a different reason than the comment gave.

**The r = q case, closed on a second pass, and it closes toward the framework.** The theorem excludes r = q, where translations may sit in Γ/Γ₁, Sylow-q is not forced cyclic, and the diagonal step is unavailable — so the sub-case looked like the dangerous one, with an unbounded F = 2 class potentially reaching ~n²/4. It is in fact the *most* constrained case: at 2 ≤ F < q the twist is trivial outright, T\* is the diagonal C_q, and some class has only F·q = |O| pairs (Lemma D2q of Part D2). The mechanism generalises S10's F = 1 normality argument and replaces it: **linear parts at r = q have order dividing q − 1, so they are q′-elements and the top q-group forces them down into Γ₁ beside the translations they scale, where [Γ₁, Γ₁] ⊆ Γ₂ — a p-group — cannot hold the resulting order-q commutator (λ − 1)·𝟙.** At r ≠ q the twist is a q-power living a layer above the translations, the commutator lands harmlessly, and the twist survives; that asymmetry is why r ≠ q needs the C(F,2)·r counting argument and r = q does not. One principle now covers both, and is the form worth remembering: *a twist forced into the same abelian layer as the translations it scales must be trivial.*

Three computations pin it down, in `a18_rq_verify.py`. Exhaustively at (F,q) = (2,5): all 252 subgroups of the order-200 monomial-with-swap group on 10 points, with every q = 5-chained transitive subgroup at m\* = 5 ≤ F·q, every q ∈ {2,3}-chained one obeying the r ≠ q bound on the same data, and all five rank-2 translation groups (orders 50, 100, 200) **chainless for every top prime** — independent translations are inadmissible outright rather than merely dominated. At (3,7), the shape the proof has to work hardest to kill: a genuine rank-2 eigenvector module T\* = ⟨𝟙, (1,2,4)⟩ whose block 3-cycle conjugates w to w² at common multiplier 4, closing at order 147 — and admitting no q = 7 chain, so the commutator obstruction is real and not an artefact of F = 2. And two controls: untwisted C₂₁ has a chain with m\* = 21 = F·q exactly, so the bound is attained; adding a multiplier of order 3 destroys the chain, which is the twist-collapse observed directly.

**Consequences.** Lemma D2 holds with **no r ≠ q hypothesis**, and the C(F,2)·r same-position bound transfers to r = q as well once the linear parts are trivial, so `a18_verify.py`'s range check — which had always iterated over all primes r — was correct as computed and needed no rerun. S10 returns to settled at every F, now as a corollary of D2q rather than as a separate normality argument, and its census rows lose the F = 1 qualifier. The top entry of the residual-risk ranking retires with the item; the ranking's new head is Part E's realisability construction (T2), which is where the ledger's pattern had already pointed.

**The right property is 2-homogeneity, and naming it as 2-transitivity would have hidden a family.** What the same-position class costs is the permuter's minimum orbital on *unordered* pairs, so 2-homogeneity is what maximises it. The two part company at degree ≡ 3 (mod 4), where C_F ⋊ C_{(F−1)/2} is 2-homogeneous without being 2-transitive — and is *more* chain-friendly, needing only (F−1)/2 to be a prime power. At F = 7 that gives C₇ ⋊ C₃ with q = 3, where the full AGL(1,7) = C₇ ⋊ C₆ is rejected by the chain. The resulting witness is **sharper than the n = 85 one**: seven fused 13-blocks at **n = 91**, order 819, Γ₁ = C₉₁ cyclic normal, Γ/Γ₁ of order 9, orbitals 273 (×3) and 819 (×4), so **m\* = 3|O|**. An argument phrased via 2-transitivity would have looked for AGL(1,7), found the chain rejects it, and missed the family. The domination bound C(F,2)·r is unaffected — 2-homogeneity is exactly what saturates it — but the set of F at which the shape is buildable is larger than a 2-transitive reading suggests.

**Verification artefacts.** `a18_verify.py`, four passes (both witnesses' orbitals and chains, the permuter's 2-homogeneity, and the range check), exits nonzero on failure; added to R1, because its range half expires silently on any table extension. `a18_rq_verify.py`, nine static checks for the r = q half; nothing in it depends on the table, so it stays out of R1 and needs one run per environment.

## 2. The k = 3 Galois admissibility predicate was too narrow (now A19)

**What it used to say.** `three-uniform-note.md` §2.2.2's Oliver-constrained corollary held that the Galois gain requires **a = q^e with q ≥ 5**, on the reasoning that Frobenius acts nontrivially on C_d for d > 1, so C_a cannot join the cyclic layer and must be the top q-group entire. It offered a = 35 as a case the unconstrained criterion admits and Oliver's condition does not.

**Why it was wrong.** "The generator cannot join the middle layer" does not give "all of C_a sits on top". A subgroup C_{a′} ≤ C_a acting trivially on C_d may join it, leaving only C_{a/a′} to be a q-group. Its own stated test case is the counterexample: **a = 35, d = 31, c = 2³⁵** — since 31 | 2⁵ − 1 divides 2^{35/7} − 1, take a′ = 7 in the cyclic layer, where C₃₁ × C₇ ≅ C₂₁₇ is cyclic, leaving Γ/Γ₁ ≅ C₅. A genuine chain with top prime 5 and gain factor 5.

**What replaced it.** The layer split: **∃ a′ | a with d | 2^{a/a′} − 1, gcd(d, a′) = 1, and a/a′ a prime power.** The old condition is the a′ = 1 branch alone.

**Why it mattered enough to be an item.** The error under-counts admissible Galois blocks, and §5.8 of that document establishes that a k = 3 scoring which under-credits the Galois part is *not an upper bound at all* — the reverse of the k = 2 situation. Nothing was running on the predicate, which is why the fix was free; the item exists so it stays fixed when a k = 3 enumerator is built. Unaffected either way: characteristic 2, gcd(a, 6) = 1, gcd(d, 6) = 1, the q | a top-prime coupling of §4.3, and the O(n/log n) escape count.

## 2.1 A19 implemented, and two things the implementation settled

The corrected predicate of §2 was implemented as `k3_galois.py` rather than left as prose, on the principle that a shape-space predicate is cheapest to fix before code depends on it. Implementing it surfaced two distinctions the prose had left implicit and one bound that changes how urgent the item was.

**Gain and top prime are not the same quantity.** The theorem's gain is **lpf(a)**; the split's top prime is **q = lpf(a/a′)**. They agree exactly when a is a prime power — which is why the superseded reading could conflate them without visible damage, and why conflating them again would have been the natural next error inside an enumerator.

**The twist selects the top prime at fixed block size.** At a = 35, the twist d = 31 splits with a′ = 7 and gives q = 5; d = 127 splits with a′ = 5 and gives q = 7. Same block, same gain 5, different top prime. Since §4.3 of `three-uniform-note.md` couples q to every foreign block in the configuration — each needing q | r − 1 — that is usable freedom, and it simply does not exist under the naive predicate, where a = q^e determines q.

**The first affected n is 2³⁵, which downgrades the urgency while confirming the substance.** The block sizes the correction adds are the a coprime to 6 that are not prime powers — {35, 55, 65, …}, all with lpf(a) = 5, hence all low-gain. But the shape is n = c = 2^a, so the block size *determines* n and the error is not diluted across many values: at n = 2³⁵ ≈ 3.4 × 10¹⁰ the naive predicate credits gain 1 against a true gain of 5, making the bound a factor of 5 too small and hence not an upper bound. So the error was real, was of the dangerous kind (§5.8: under-crediting at k = 3 breaks the bound rather than loosening it), and was never within reach of any computation. Worth recording as a calibration point: **"dangerous direction" and "reachable" are independent axes**, and this item was the first while not being the second.

## 2.2 T5: Lemma C falsified, replaced by a coupling theorem

**What Lemma C used to say.** *A twist of order d in the cyclic layer of a p-characteristic part shares no prime with any outside block of prime size r: gcd(d, r) = 1.* Its status line read "proved when the block has prime size, open at a > 1", the open case being that a top element may act through the Galois part of ΓL(1, p^a), whose induced power map has q-power order just as the foreign multiplier does — so the conjugation argument does not close.

**It is false, and at a = 1 as well as a > 1.** Two Oliver groups, both machine-verified:

> - **n = 28 = 25 + 3 (a = 2).** 𝔽₂₅ with translations C₅², beside 𝔽₃. One cyclic-layer element acts as multiplication by ω of order 3 on 𝔽₂₅ and as +1 on 𝔽₃; one top element acts as Frobenius x ↦ x⁵ there and as negation here, so both projections give z ↦ z⁻¹ and conjugation closes. |Γ| = 150, Γ₂ = C₅², Γ₁/Γ₂ ≅ C₃, Γ/Γ₁ ≅ C₂. Twist order and foreign prime both 3.
> - **n = 10 = 7 + 3 (a = 1).** The share exists at a *prime* block when the outside part is untwisted: order 21, Γ₂ = C₇, layer C₃, trivial top.

The second is the more interesting failure. The a = 1 proof was **correct** — it shows that a share forces the outside twist to be trivial, since a top element inducing a nontrivial foreign twist would contradict the layer's single power map. The *statement* recorded above it claimed gcd(d, r) = 1, which is strictly stronger and which nothing established. The discrepancy survived because the statement was only ever tested against configurations with a twisted foreign part, which is exactly the case its proof did cover.

**What replaced it: the twist–foreign coupling.** If r | d then every multiplier induced on the outside part lies in ⟨p mod r⟩, so the outside twist order satisfies **t | ord_r(p) | a**, and the part carries an intra class of at most min(r·ord_r(p), C(r,2)) ≤ n·log₂n. The proof projects the layer's single power map to both parts — Frobenius exponent on one side, multiplier on the other — and uses D2q's commutator step to show cyclic-layer elements induce trivial multipliers on the outside part. At a = 1 it reads t | 1, recovering what the old proof actually proved.

The coupling is **tight and rigid**, which is what makes it a theorem about realisability rather than a bound: at (c, r) = (16, 5), ord₅(2) = 4 and the share is realised with outside twist exactly 4 (|Γ| = 320, layer C₅, top C₄); pairing the wrong Frobenius exponent with the multiplier — Frob² against a multiplier of order 4 — fails to close, the closure containing the pure matching twist and the pure outside translation separately, so its Sylow 5-subgroup is C₅ × C₅ and no chain exists.

**Corollary C′ and what T5 becomes.** A sharing configuration is excluded wherever δ(n)·C(n,2) > n·log₂n — from **n ≥ 763** at the ladder's δ ≥ 0.02516, with direct scoring clearing the whole computed range below that (worst ratio 0.7000 at n = 15). Beyond 10⁶ the exclusion needs only δ(n) ≫ log n / n, the weakest arithmetic input the framework has yet leaned on.

The consequence for the certificates is the point of the item. Condition (4)'s foreign-prime strip is **not** necessary on admissible configurations — the witnesses are admissible and violate it — but **is** necessary among configurations scoring above n·log₂n, which is the only regime the certificates evaluate. So the strip is justified **at every a**, on coupling-plus-threshold rather than on an unconditional lemma, and T5 reduces from a mathematical gap to a code change plus a rerun over the 53,807 proper-prime-power branches it affects. The remaining fallback residue is the q = 2 and large-e pinning cases, with no Lemma-C caveat attached. `t5_verify.py` reproduces the witnesses, the tightness, the negative control and the range check, and its last pass is range-scoped, so it joins R1 beside `a18_verify.py`.

## 5.1 The hypothesis taxonomy, and a k = 3 correction it turned up

`orbital-evasiveness-notes.md` §1 now carries a **"Which hypothesis is doing which work"** table: rows vary the group class (Oliver → solvable) and the arity (k = 2 → k = 3) one at a time, columns report what happens to the shifted-prime condition, the shape space, fusion, balance and the supply of full-density blocks. The primary documents each declare which row they are (`enumeration-proof.md` and `aod` are row 1, `solvable-relaxation.md` row 2, `three-uniform-note.md` row 3; row 4 is unworked and says so).

**The finding the table forced.** The two axes turn out to be orthogonal — the group axis controls η alone, the arity axis controls the full-density block alone — and checking the second of those exposed an error introduced earlier in this same session. While renaming 2-transitivity to 2-homogeneity, the parallel claim at k = 3 was rewritten from "solvable 3-transitive groups do not exist above degree 4" (true) to "solvable 3-homogeneous groups do not exist above degree 4" (**false**). The k-homogeneity trap bites at both arities:

> The solvable 3-homogeneous degrees are **{3, 4, 5, 8, 32}**, all prime powers and all Oliver: C₃, A₄, AGL(1,5), AGL(1,8) and AΓL(1,32). The last two are *regular* on triples — |AGL(1,8)| = 56 = C(8,3) and |AΓL(1,32)| = 4960 = C(32,3) — hence 3-homogeneous without being 3-transitive; AGL(1,5) is the same phenomenon at 10 triples. Degrees 6 and 7 fail (2/6/6/6 and 14/21). So **δ₃ = 1 at all five**, and S1's analogue at k = 3 is non-empty — merely finite.

The finiteness is sharp rather than generous, which is the part worth keeping: |AΓL(1,c)| = c(c−1)log₂c against C(c,3) ≈ c³/6 permits equality only to c = 32 and fails from c = 64; at the single intermediate degree the order bound does permit, c = 16 with 960 ≥ 560, the group is not 3-homogeneous (orbits 80 and 480). So above degree 5 the list is exactly {8, 32}, and at 8 and 32 the order bound is met with *equality* — those two are regular on triples, the family stopping because it runs out of room with its last members fitting exactly. Corrected in `three-uniform-note.md` §3.1 and four other sites there, in `aod` §5's contingency box, and in the hypothesis table; verified by `khomog_verify.py`.

**Two corrections, not one, and the second is the more embarrassing.** The first was introduced *by* a correct fix — renaming 2-transitivity to 2-homogeneity at k = 2 — applied one level up without rechecking the classification there. The second is that the replacement box first written asserted the list was {8, 32}, when `three-uniform-note.md` §3.1 **already quoted Kantor's theorem correctly**, including A₄ at n = 4 and AGL(1,5) at n = 5, and its own S1 row already said {5, 8, 32}. A new claim was written into a document that already contained the right one, and the two sat three sections apart contradicting each other. Three lessons now, the last two cheap. A correction propagated by analogy inherits the confidence of the case it was verified on — the k = 2 rename was verified, the k = 3 rename was not, and it was wrong — so a renaming sweep is a new claim per site, not one claim applied many times. And **before writing a classification into a document, grep that document for the classification**: the correct statement was already there, and the contradiction would have surfaced in one command. Search-before-assert costs nothing and would have caught this at the point of introduction rather than two turns later. Third, from Lemma C: **read each lemma's statement against its own proof's conclusion**, independently of whether the proof is believed. Lemma C's a = 1 proof was sound and its statement was stronger than what the proof delivered, and no amount of scrutiny directed at the proof would have found that — only a comparison of the two.

**A checker artefact, not a defect — and one whose obvious diagnosis was wrong.** `validate_table.py` reported one group A failure, at n = 2561, where μ/C(n,2) = 49/640 = 0.0765625 is an **exact tie at the sixth decimal**, the only one in 2,186 rows; re-derivation from the witness passes at that n.

The natural reading is "the check needs a rounding tolerance". It had one, and the correct one: `abs(delta - B/C) > 5e-7`, a half-ulp at six decimals. The real cause is that **the comparison is evaluated in floating point at its own boundary**: a correctly rounded tie sits exactly *on* 5e-7, and `0.076563 − 0.0765625` in doubles is `5.000000000005e-07`, a few ulps above. Any tolerance set to an exact half-ulp fails this way at precisely the inputs it exists to admit, since ties are the only values that reach the boundary and floating point settles them by accident of representation. Widening the tolerance would have hidden that while weakening the check.

Fixed by comparing in exact rational arithmetic against the stored string's own precision — |stored − B/C| ≤ ½·10⁻ᵏ with k read off the string, which also stops the check loosening silently if the writer's format changes. Verified at 0 mismatches over the table plus eleven behavioural cases (tie accepted either way, one-in-the-last-place errors rejected both directions, truncation rejected, 4- and 8-decimal strings handled at their own precision). Recorded as A20.

*The generalisable point*, and the reason this earned a paragraph rather than a line: **a tolerance equal to the exact boundary of the property it tests is a tolerance that fails on the boundary cases**. The fix is to move the comparison into arithmetic that has no boundary error, not to move the boundary.

*On the checkpoint matcher's false positives.* `check_doc_figures.py` PASS 1 flagged 24 figures as matching an old checkpoint; **most are coincidences** — `2,212` in `small-degree-computation.md` is the n = 12 battery's class count, not a table frontier; `20.6` and `20.8` there are VF2 inference rates; `20.4` in the notes is a twist-order share; `0.400000` is the density at n = 6. The tool says as much in its own banner, but the ratio of coincidence to signal is high enough at this table size that the pass is worth reading with the surrounding sentence every time, never from the figure alone.

## 5.3 T5's coding residue, and a threshold that is not vacuous

Revisiting T5 after the R1 runs turned up two things the item's own write-up had wrong, both in the direction of overstating how routine the remaining work is.

**The threshold T5 stated is both wrong at small n and unnecessary.** T5 said the strip is justified at every a because "the certificates evaluate against thresholds of order δ·C(n,2) ≫ n·log₂n". Asymptotically true; false in the table — **B(n) > n·log₂n fails at 34 values, all n ≤ 117**. That looked like it forced a small-n carve-out. Reading `fb_common.py` showed the carve-out is an artefact of bounding crudely: Corollary C′'s sharing bound is **local to (p, a, r)**, namely min(r·ord_r(p), C(r,2)), so the licence is simply *that* < B, with **no threshold on n at all**. The crude global form is implied by it and strictly weaker. So the right lesson is not "mind the small n" but **do not globalise a local bound and then defend the globalised version**.

The a = 1 case falls out uniformly: the coupling forces ord_r(p) = 1 there, so the bound is r and the licence reads r < B — precisely the condition the shipped unconditional a = 1 strip was assuming without stating. It holds at every tabulated n, so nothing was ever wrong, but it is a condition and the code now tests it rather than assuming it.

**The rerun cannot change the verdict, and knowing that changes what it is for.** `fallback_cert.py` reports 0 candidates at all 2,186 values, with and without theorems, against the *current* code — whose a = 1 scoping makes condition (4) strictly **weaker** than the lifted version. Strengthening a necessary condition can only discard more branches, so an already-empty candidate list stays empty. The rerun is a regression check on the implementation, not a step in the proof.

Which has a practical consequence worth recording: **a silent over-strip produces exactly the same clean output as a correct run**, because the list is empty either way. So the gate is written as an assertion that fails loudly when the strip fires unlicensed, not as a condition that quietly declines to fire, and `set_strip_trace()` records the complementary event — every (p, a, r, B, bound) where the gate declined. Verification that only observes the candidate list cannot distinguish the two cases.

**The change, measured on a synthetic grid — and then on the real enumeration, where it evaporates.** Differential testing over **1,043,424** `single_part_ok` calls: 121 verdicts differ, all in the strictly-stronger direction, 0 unsound, assertion never fired; on a (p, a, r, B) grid restricted to r | p^a − 1 the new gate strips at 245 of 324 points the old one declined. That looked substantive. Instrumenting the certificate's *actual* branch enumeration says otherwise: **74 strip decisions are reached over the whole table, all at a = 1, none at a > 1, and 0 verdicts change.** The a > 1 branches — 129,878 entering `pair_candidates`, 120,389 surviving the divisibility and dispatch filters — **all die at the foreign cap `orb(r, t) < B` before condition (4) is evaluated at all.** The lift is correct and inert.

**A figure that had to be retired rather than re-measured.** T5 recorded that the strip changes condition (4)'s verdict on 630,477 branches at n ≤ 2000, 53,807 of them with c a proper prime power, and I had listed re-measuring it as outstanding work. It is not reproducible against the current file: condition (4) is evaluated **30 times** at n ≤ 2000 and 74 times over the whole table, with **zero** proper-prime-power evaluations at either. The number measures some other quantity or a pre-tightening version of the code, and carrying it forward as "wants re-measuring" would have preserved a claim that no longer means anything.

*Both discoveries came from the same move*: instrumenting the decision site rather than reading the output. The certificate's output is identical — 0 candidates, both modes — before and after the change, and would have been identical had the change been wrong.

*Generalising, since this is the second time in the session:* the R1 runs answer whether the pipeline is self-consistent, and are nearly silent about whether a strengthening is *licensed*. Both the A20 boundary bug and this gate live in the space of things that pass every check while being wrong, and both were found by asking what the check would look like if the code were wrong rather than by reading the check's output.

## 5.4 The mod-24 ceilings were not the joint optimum

Working out why `ladder_verify.py`'s missing even F mattered led to a substantive correction in `arithmetic-of-density.md` §3.3.5. **The derivation, measurements and diagnosis are in `fusion-count-ceilings.md`, kept as a separate note**; this entry records only what happened and why the documents changed.

**The finding.** §3.3.5 fixed a rung per residue class from a condition mod 8, then optimised η within it. The correct object is a joint optimum over admissible (F, η) pairs, because F and η are coupled: cap_F(η) = η/(1 + √(Fη))² decreases in F at fixed η, so fusing is worth doing only when a larger F moves r into a residue carrying a better η. At odd n the fusion count must be **even** — c odd and r an odd prime force F·c even — and **F = 4 beats F = 2 at four residues**, because 4c ≡ 4 rather than 6 (mod 8) sends r to 3 (mod 8) and removes the 2-adic cut. Ceilings at 7, 15 rise to 1/9 and at 11, 23 to 7 − 4√3; the global constant moves from (5 − 2√6)/2 = 0.050510 to **7 − 4√3 = 0.071797**, and its extremal class coarsens from n ≡ 23 (mod 24) alone to all of **n ≡ 11 (mod 12)**, since with F = 4 the mod-8 condition drops out and only the ℓ = 3 obstruction remains.

**The root cause was a false statement, not an unexplored case.** The census S7 row read *"O(n/log n) odd values where F·c even forces c = 2^a"*. That implication holds only for **odd** F, where Fc = odd·odd is odd; the sentence quantified over all F ≥ 3 while its proof covered half the partition. Measured: 500 of 500 odd n in [50000, 51000] admit n = 4c + r with c and r both odd primes — a full Hardy–Littlewood system, not O(log n) block sizes per n. The same restriction appears in §4.3's fate-(iii) list, which enumerates "F = 3 or 5" explicitly.

**Why even F was excluded in the first place**, which is the more useful lesson: the block-permuting group's order must be coprime to what else the cyclic layer carries, and for even F the prime 2 must not divide the *twist* — but c is odd, so 2 always divides c − 1. Reading that as "even F is inadmissible" is the error; the twist is **cut** to the odd part of c − 1, costing a factor of 2 and often still worth it. `ladder_verify.py` encoded the wrong reading as the guard `(c-1) % qF == 0 → continue`.

**Nothing computed changed.** `mu_enumerate_v2.py`'s `parts_for` implements the correct reading and has always searched even F — the v4 table has such winners (n = 1175 wins with `1x619* + 4x139`). So B(n), μ(n) = B(n), the collapse certificate and every tabulated value stand. The error was confined to the prose and the ladder script, which is why it survived: **the enumerator and the census S7 row had been in direct contradiction for as long as both existed**, and diffing them would have caught it at any time. That is a cheaper check than re-reading either in isolation, and worth adding to the review repertoire.

**A follow-on that caught an error of mine.** Asking whether 11 and 23 should share a table row turned up that they should — the row entries are identical, and the table already groups residues whose foreign residue differs. But checking what *distinguishes* them showed the second-best shape at class 23 is the **F = 6** rung at 0.066987, not S4's 0.050510. I had used the latter in §7's disjunction-collapse argument, which put the usable margin at ε < 0.0213; the true figure is **ε < 0.0048**, a quarter of that, and a real weakening of that route. Corrected, with the reason recorded in the table's commentary, since any argument needing the runner-up rather than the winner has to split the merged row.

**And it dissolved a long-standing observation.** Low-δ values were thought to concentrate at n ≡ 23 (mod 24) because that class had the lowest ceiling. With the ceilings equal, the concentration turns out to have been mostly a **selection effect**: the old worklist was thresholded at 0.050510, which *was* class 23's own ceiling, so 23 appeared on merely falling short of its cap while 11 had to fall 25% below a higher one. Regenerated at the shared threshold the split is 1823 : 1824. Direct measurements agree — medians 0.07047 vs 0.07035, fractions below cap 64.6% vs 65.6%, representation counts 7.66 vs 6.84 and never zero. A residual tail asymmetry survives (47 of the 60 lowest values across both classes are at 23, about 3σ), which is worth not claiming to be zero, but it is nothing like the factor of 1200 the old worklist showed. **The data was never evidence about caps; it was evidence about where a threshold had been drawn.**

**A check that would have caught it, now added.** `validate_table.py` was printing `S7 125 (5.7%)` in its census-counts line on every run, a few lines below a census row asserting that shape wins nowhere — so the contradiction was in the output the whole time, unread. The naive fix (assert the count is 0) does not work, because `wins → 0` is an asymptotic limit and S1/S2 win half the values in range while legitimately claiming it. What a density-zero supply argument *does* imply is a **declining winner share**, so group B now checks exactly that for the shapes claiming a vanishing share, and fails only on growth. Confirmed as a regression test: putting `S7` back into the zero-share set makes it fail with `S7 4.1%→7.6%` against `S2 45.2%→29.3%`.

**Consequences beyond the table.** The regenerated worklist grows from 16 entries to 3,693 at 10⁵, because it is thresholded at the asymptotic constant and that constant rose — so the list is now much weaker as a "compute B(n) here" ranking, and the 1/50 question is better read from the separate `--floor` count. The three-part-family proportions in §3.9.1 remain correct *about that family* and are now scoped as such rather than presented as class ceilings. `three-uniform-note.md`'s k = 3 table has four rows whose κ_c = 2 entries were recomputed from verified formulas and whose κ_c = 3 entries are flagged as needing the k = 3 twist analysis redone at F = 4.

## 5.5 The 10⁶ ladder run, and the conjecture sharpened to 1/25

The full scan completed in 12,458s. Results and what they licensed:

**The verified floor over 10⁶ is 0.04453, attained at n = 11183 = 53·211**, and it is fixed by n = 20,000 — over the remaining 980,000 values nothing undercuts it. Since the ladder is a lower bound at every n, this proves **δ(n) ≥ 1/25 for every composite non-prime-power n ≤ 10⁶**, with 11% of margin. The conjecture in `aod` §5 moves from **1/50 to 1/25**, a 2× strengthening, with 1/50 retained in the surrounding commentary because the gap between the two is itself the interesting fact.

**Why the old constant looks so loose now, and why the new one probably is too.** 1/50 was set when the observed floor was near 0.026 and falling with every extension, so it was a modest margin under a moving target. The target then reversed, and every historical fall turned out to be a scoring deficiency rather than a property of μ — the pre-repair shape space read n = 2291 at 0.037524 against a true 0.066767, and the pre-repair ladder read n = 8927 at 0.02516 where the corrected families lift it off the worklist entirely. **The drift is one-directional for a structural reason**: B(n) is a maximum over admissible configurations, so a missing shape can only depress it, and a missing shape leaves no witness — nothing in the pipeline can detect one — whereas an over-credit is caught by re-deriving μ from the recorded witness. Errors that survive to be found later are therefore biased toward those that lower the floor. That is now recorded in §5 as a reason to read 1/25 as likely loose in its turn.

**Two downstream consequences worth noting.** The part-count cap of Proposition F.1 tightens from k ≤ 7 to **k ≤ 5** at the conjectured floor, which materially narrows the search §6 sizes. And Open Problem 8(b)'s hard branches become unreachable: at δ ≥ 0.04453 the cap is s ≤ 3.74, so **s = 4 and s = 5 — the branches with no theorem — cannot arise anywhere below 10⁶**, leaving E.4's collapse of s = 3 as the whole remaining content.

**Confirmations of the F = 4 work.** The worklist splits **23,475 : 23,201** between n ≡ 11 and n ≡ 23 (mod 24), a 50.2/49.7 dead heat across 46,722 entries — about as clean a confirmation of the merged §3.3.5 row as available. And the per-residue min-ratio column puts 23 at 0.620 of its cap and 11 at 0.671, against a spread from 0.327 (class 16) to 0.671 across all twenty-four: the two lowest-cap classes are mid-range in *relative* shortfall, so the low absolute floor at 23 comes from its cap being lowest, not from a family the ladder misses there.

*Two methodological notes on the untruncated series.* It tracks the clamped block floor closely, which confirms the clamp is not hiding a lower region — what it was added for. It is **not** evidence that no family is missing, since both series run the same four families; the evidence for that is the per-residue ratio column above, which compares like with like. And the exact coincidence at the 800k block is **not** evidence of anything: the series samples one in four of classes 11 and 23, so it agrees with the block floor precisely when the block's minimising n happens to be sampled, and one such coincidence in ten blocks is slightly below what that rate alone predicts. The informative fact in that column is that all ten block minima lie in class 11 or 23 — eight at 23, two at 11 — which is read off the block floors alone.

## 5.6 The η column derived rather than sampled

The ceiling table's efficiencies were measured over a sample of n; they are now derived from congruences, and the two agree at all thirty-six (class, F) cells. `aod` §3.3.4a carries the derivation, `eta_derive.py` checks both sides, and T6 narrows to the supply hypothesis plus three scope limits.

**The derivation.** With c ≡ 3 (mod 4), F·c is determined mod 8 — 6, 4, 2 at F = 2, 4, 6 — so r is too, and the odd twist gives η₂ = 2^(1−v) with v = v₂(r − 1) read off r mod 8. Independently, a forced 3 | r − 1 cuts η by 3, and whether it is forced depends on F mod 3: for F ≢ 0 the choice of c mod 3 steers r off 1 (mod 3) unless n ≡ 2 (mod 3), while F = 6 has r ≡ n (mod 3) forced, cutting at n ≡ 1 and making the shape non-existent at n ≡ 0.

**The first attempt failed at three cells, and the failure was informative.** At r ≡ 1 (mod 8) the mod-8 rule gives only v ≥ 3; the exact value needs mod 16, where 4c ≡ 12 is again forced, so r ≡ n − 12 (mod 16) — and **n mod 16 is not determined by n mod 24**. Classes 5, 13 and 21 split at F = 4. That looked at first like a threat to the mod-24 keying of the whole table, and is not: all three are cells where F = 4 is not the optimum, and the four classes where it is — 7, 11, 15, 23 — have r ≡ 3 or 7 (mod 8), hence v = 1 exactly, pinned at the smallest value with no deeper modulus able to move it. **The keying holds because the binding cells are precisely the ones where the 2-adic valuation bottoms out**, which is a better reason than the table previously had.

**What the derivation buys, stated narrowly.** It removes the sampling: the η values are congruence consequences, checkable by hand, and no longer rest on 30–60 sampled n per class. It does *not* touch the supply hypothesis — that primes r = 2^v·q^e + 1 occur near the balance point in the needed density is Bateman–Horn, as everywhere in §3. The measurement asked whether such an r turns up in practice; the derivation asks whether one is congruence-obstructed. The second was the part in doubt, and it is now closed.

*Method note.* The measurement and the derivation are independent computations of the same thirty-six numbers, so their agreement is the T1 item-3 check applied deliberately rather than stumbled into — and it caught my transcription error in the measured table on the way, three cells having been mistyped from an earlier run.
